Transcript // Writing
Conquest Mother
Atop her throne of briars and bone, The queen reigns, her conquest hushed. My hand, torn by her sweet lament, Bears a nail driven through stone and silken rust. A giant slumbers in the withered plains, Her grave a sprawl of splintered cross and shale. From her spine, a lineage of grief unfurls, Her cloak, a shroud, exacts the toll of farewell. Mothers to our toppled groves, they stand— Regret, a quickening pang, claws at the end. In anguish's bloom, we cling to fallen boughs, Our roots entwined with hers, unyielding, condemned.
